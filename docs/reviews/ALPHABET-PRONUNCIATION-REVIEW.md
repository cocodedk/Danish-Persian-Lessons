# Alphabet pronunciation review

Status: researched implementation candidate. Native standard-Tehrani and Danish review still blocks
release approval.

## Plain decisions

- Persian uses spellings from Arabic, but modern Persian does not keep every Arabic sound.
- ث، س، ص all sound like `s`; ذ، ز، ض، ظ all sound like `z`; ت، ط both sound like `t`; ح، ه
  both sound like `h`. The app names the spellings and says that their sound is the same.
- The two h letters use their full school names: `ح جیمی` / he-ye djimi and `ه دو چشم` /
  he-ye do tjeshm.
- ق and غ share one Tehrani sound. It is often more closed first in a word and softer between vowels.
- ع is not the base sound of Danish æ, ø, å, or y. In Persian it may make a small break or no sound;
  the vowel heard in a word is learned with that word.
- ا, و, ه, and ی keep their word-based jobs visible. The keyboard says `vokal`, `v/u`, `h/e`, and
  `j/i` instead of giving one false fixed sound.

## Sources checked

- The University of Texas [system of transcription](https://sites.la.utexas.edu/persian_online_resources/system-of-transcription/)
  states the Persian same-sound groups and explains that Persian has no Arabic pharyngeal sound for ع.
- Its [writing-system guide](https://sites.la.utexas.edu/persian_online_resources/the-writing-system/)
  explains that Arabic loan spellings are read with the Persian sound system.
- Its [vowel guide](https://sites.la.utexas.edu/persian_online_resources/phonology/phonology-1/),
  [long-vowel guide](https://sites.la.utexas.edu/persian_online_resources/phonology/long-vowels/),
  [final-he guide](https://sites.la.utexas.edu/persian_online_resources/phonology/silent-letters-silent-and-consonantal/),
  and [silent-vav guide](https://sites.la.utexas.edu/persian_online_resources/phonology/silent-letters-vav/)
  support the word-based roles.
- Encyclopaedia Iranica's [Early New Persian review](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/persian-language-1-early-new-persian/?generate_pdf=1)
  records the Tehrani positional pattern for ق and غ.
- Den Danske Ordbog's [Danish sound table](https://ordnet.dk/ddo/hjaelp/udtale/) lists æ, ø, å,
  and y as vowel sounds; they are not one sound and are not derived from ع.

No synthetic or unreviewed audio was added. The recording queue remains open for human speakers and
reviewers.
